What's left after you subtract the market

Why I became obsessed with the part of a stock's move that no model explains.

DRAFT — 아래는 이 저장소의 프로젝트를 바탕으로 한 예시 초안입니다. 실제 경험에 맞게 다시 써주세요.

On an earnings day, a stock can jump eight percent and tell you almost nothing. If the whole market rallied, if its sector rallied, if small caps or momentum names had a good week — how much of that eight percent was really about the company? TODO: 이 질문이 처음 떠오른 구체적인 순간

That question became the Earnings Idio Score. I regressed each stock's earnings-window returns on five factors — market, sector, size, value and momentum — and kept only the residual. What remained was, in a rough sense, the market's verdict on the company itself.

The interesting part of a return is the part you can't explain away.

The same idea turned out to apply to people. When I started tracking how top active-ETF managers changed their holdings, most day-to-day weight changes were just prices drifting. Only after subtracting that drift could I see the trades a manager actually chose to make. TODO: 막혔던 지점, 틀렸던 가정, 발견한 것
